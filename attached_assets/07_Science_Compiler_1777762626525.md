# Science Compiler
## Make All Human Knowledge Executable and Reproducible

---

## The Core Insight

There is a profound irony at the foundation of human knowledge: the system we use to generate and store knowledge is itself fundamentally broken.

Consider what should be the simplest thing in science: you read a study that says "X causes Y." You want to know: is that true? Is that still true? Has anyone replicated it? What do other studies say? What are the confounding variables? What is the size of the effect?

To answer these questions today, you must:
- Find the original paper (often paywalled)
- Find the studies that cite it (fragmented across multiple databases)
- Manually read and compare dozens of papers
- Check whether the original data is still available (usually it is not)
- Attempt to replicate the analysis if you want to verify it (requires the same software environment, which may no longer exist)
- Synthesize conflicting findings yourself

This process takes days. Most people do not do it. They cite the summary of the summary of the paper, and the knowledge degrades with each retelling like a game of telephone.

The Science Compiler reengineers this from the ground up: a platform where every claim in science is linked to its evidence, that evidence is executable and reproducible, conflicting claims are explicitly tracked, and AI synthesizes the current state of knowledge on any question in real time.

You do not search for answers. You query the compiled knowledge base and get an answer with full provenance, uncertainty quantification, and links to every piece of evidence that supports or contradicts it.

This is what Google should have been. A compiler for truth.

## The Reproducibility Crisis

The scale of the problem cannot be overstated. A 2015 landmark study by the Open Science Collaboration found that only 36% of psychological studies replicated successfully. In cancer biology, the Reproducibility Project found that only 11 of 53 landmark cancer studies could be successfully reproduced.

This is not primarily a fraud problem. It is an infrastructure problem. Studies fail to replicate for reasons that have nothing to do with scientific fraud:

- **The data is not available**: The original researchers cannot find the raw data 5 years later. It was stored on a hard drive that failed, or in a format that cannot be read.
- **The analysis environment is not reproducible**: The code was written for a software version that no longer exists. The packages have changed. The random seed was not recorded.
- **The methodology was described ambiguously**: The paper describes the method in general terms. A different researcher interprets it differently and gets a different result.
- **Publication bias**: Studies that find positive results get published. Studies that find nothing, or find contradictions, sit in filing cabinets.

The result: the scientific literature is a swamp of unreliable claims, and nobody has built the infrastructure to drain it.

## Why Now

**Large language models can read and reason about scientific papers.** A capable LLM can read a paper, extract its claims, identify its methodology, and evaluate the strength of its evidence. This was not possible five years ago.

**Open science is gaining momentum.** PubMed Central, arXiv, bioRxiv, OSF (Open Science Framework) — more papers than ever are open-access. More researchers are sharing data and code. The raw material for a knowledge compiler is becoming available.

**Semantic web technologies have matured.** Knowledge graphs, ontologies, and structured data formats for scientific knowledge have been developed over decades. These are the scaffolding the Science Compiler runs on.

**Compute costs make large-scale paper processing economical.** Processing millions of papers — extracting claims, building knowledge graphs, computing contradictions — would have cost billions of dollars a decade ago. Today it costs millions. In five years it will cost hundreds of thousands.

**The market for scientific intelligence is enormous and growing.** Pharmaceutical companies, venture capital funds, government research agencies, and academic institutions all need better ways to navigate the scientific literature. They are already paying billions for inferior solutions (scientific publishing subscriptions, research services, manual literature reviews).

## What This Actually Is

The Science Compiler has four components:

### Component 1: The Knowledge Ingestion Engine

A pipeline that continuously processes the entire body of scientific literature and converts it from static PDFs into a structured, queryable knowledge base.

**Paper ingestion**: Integrates with PubMed, arXiv, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar, CrossRef, and other scientific repositories. New papers are ingested within hours of publication.

**Claim extraction**: AI reads each paper and extracts structured claims:
```json
{
  "claim": "Coffee consumption reduces risk of Type 2 diabetes",
  "direction": "protective",
  "effect_size": { "value": 0.86, "ci_lower": 0.82, "ci_upper": 0.91, "unit": "relative_risk" },
  "population": "adult humans",
  "conditions": ["daily consumption > 4 cups"],
  "methodology": "meta-analysis",
  "evidence_quality": "A",
  "study_id": "pmid:12345678",
  "replication_status": "confirmed",
  "n_replications": 12,
  "conflicting_claims": ["pmid:23456789", "pmid:34567890"]
}
```

**Methodology extraction**: For every study, extract: study type (RCT, observational, meta-analysis), sample size, population characteristics, statistical methods, effect size, p-value, confidence intervals, and limitations acknowledged.

**Code and data linking**: Where code and data are publicly available (GitHub, OSF, Zenodo), link them to the paper. Make it one click to run the analysis yourself.

**Citation graph construction**: Build the full citation network — which papers cite which. Identify review papers, landmark studies, and the genealogy of ideas.

### Component 2: The Knowledge Synthesis Engine

The AI layer that synthesizes across studies to answer questions.

**Claim aggregation**: For any given claim, aggregates all supporting and contradicting evidence. Produces a meta-view:
- Number of studies supporting the claim
- Number of studies contradicting it
- Weighted average effect size (weighted by study quality and sample size)
- Current consensus status: "Well-established," "Contested," "Preliminary," "Retracted"

**Uncertainty quantification**: Every synthesized answer comes with explicit uncertainty:
- What fraction of studies agree?
- How strong is the evidence quality?
- What are the methodological concerns?
- What are the known moderating variables (the effect is true for population X but not for Y)?

**Contradiction resolution**: When studies conflict, the synthesis engine does not just average them. It identifies why they conflict: different populations, different methodologies, different time periods, different operational definitions of the same concept.

**Temporal tracking**: Science changes. The system tracks how the evidence on any given claim has evolved over time. "This claim was contested in 2015 but has been robustly confirmed by 2025."

### Component 3: The Reproducibility Runtime

The infrastructure that makes scientific analyses executable.

**Containerized analysis environments**: Every analysis code that is published with a paper is containerized in a Docker/conda environment that can be reproduced exactly, regardless of when the analysis was originally run.

**One-click replication**: Any paper with published code and data can be re-run with one click. The platform provisions the necessary compute, runs the analysis, and reports whether the result matches the published result.

**Sensitivity analysis**: Beyond simple replication, the platform runs systematic sensitivity analyses: what happens if you change one assumption? If the result changes dramatically when you change a minor methodological choice, the finding is fragile. If it is stable across assumptions, it is robust.

**Preregistration integration**: Researchers can preregister their studies — publishing their hypothesis and methodology before collecting data, preventing post-hoc interpretation of results. Preregistered studies are flagged as higher-quality evidence.

### Component 4: The Query Interface

The way scientists, policymakers, doctors, and curious people access the knowledge base.

**Natural language queries**: "What is the current evidence that social media causes depression in teenagers?" The system synthesizes the evidence and returns a structured answer:
- Current consensus: Contested
- Evidence supporting: [12 studies] — moderate effect, primarily observational
- Evidence against: [8 studies] — weak to null effect in RCTs
- Key moderating variables: age (stronger effect in pre-teens), usage type (passive vs. active), baseline mental health
- Methodological concerns: most positive studies are cross-sectional, cannot establish causation
- Highest quality evidence: [2 RCTs] — limited by short duration and lab settings
- Recommended reading: [3 review papers]

**Claim verification**: Submit any claim ("I read that X is true — is it?") and the system evaluates it against the evidence base.

**Contradiction mapping**: Show all the places in the literature where scientists disagree with each other, and explain why.

**Research gap identification**: Identify questions that have not been adequately studied — where the evidence is thin, where studies are poorly designed, where the most important moderating variables have not been examined.

## Technical Architecture

### Data Infrastructure

**Knowledge Graph**: The core data store is a property graph database (Neo4j or equivalent) where:
- Nodes: papers, claims, researchers, institutions, concepts, methods, datasets, software
- Edges: "claims," "cites," "uses-method," "contradicts," "replicates," "builds-on," "published-in"

The knowledge graph allows traversal queries that are impossible in traditional databases: "Find all claims about drug X that were made by researchers funded by the manufacturer and have not been independently replicated."

**Vector Store**: All papers and claims are embedded as vectors for semantic search and similarity queries. "Find studies that are methodologically similar to this paper."

**Time-Series Claim Store**: Tracks how claim status evolves over time. The scientific record is not static.

### AI Pipeline

**Claim Extraction Model**: Fine-tuned on a corpus of manually annotated scientific papers. Output: structured JSON of claims, methods, evidence quality scores.

**Synthesis Model**: Given a set of claims about the same topic, synthesizes them into a coherent meta-view. Critically, this is not summarization — it is structured aggregation with uncertainty quantification.

**Contradiction Detection Model**: Identifies when two claims are logically or empirically incompatible, and characterizes the nature of the disagreement.

**Evidence Quality Scorer**: Given a paper's methodology, assigns an evidence quality score based on study design, sample size, blinding, pre-registration, and replication status.

### Computation Infrastructure

Processing the entire scientific literature is a large-scale distributed computing problem:
- Approximately 50 million existing papers, 3-4 million new papers per year
- Full NLP pipeline per paper: claim extraction, entity recognition, citation analysis, embedding generation
- Continuous incremental updates as new papers are published

Estimated compute for initial full-corpus ingestion: 10,000 GPU-hours. Ongoing: 500 GPU-hours/month for new papers.

## Business Model

### Individual Researchers (Freemium)

**Free tier**: Query the knowledge base, see claim summaries, access to abstracts and public papers. Good enough for casual use.

**Pro tier ($29/month)**: Full paper access, unlimited queries, API access, research project management tools, export to citation managers.

### Institutional Licensing (Primary Revenue)

**University license ($50,000-500,000/year)**: All researchers at an institution get full access. Priced based on institution size and usage.

**Pharmaceutical/Biotech ($100,000-$2,000,000/year)**: Drug discovery requires comprehensive literature review. These companies currently pay millions for inferior manual literature review services. The Science Compiler delivers better results faster.

**Government research agencies**: NIH, NSF, DARPA, and international equivalents need tools to evaluate grant applications and track the state of funded research. Enterprise contracts.

**Think tanks and policy organizations**: Organizations that translate science into policy need rigorous, up-to-date evidence synthesis. Mid-market contracts.

### API and Developer Platform

**Science Compiler API**: Third-party developers can query the knowledge base and build applications on top. Priced per query: $0.01-$0.10 depending on query complexity.

Use cases for API:
- Medical education platforms need up-to-date evidence summaries
- Science journalism tools that fact-check scientific claims in articles
- Drug discovery software that pulls relevant literature into research pipelines
- Policy analysis tools that evaluate evidence for proposed interventions

### Publisher Partnerships

Scientific publishers (Elsevier, Springer, Wiley) have a problem: their content is increasingly bypassed by AI summaries. Partnership model: they provide full text access in exchange for integration into the Science Compiler — papers behind the paywall become accessible through the platform, with revenue sharing on the institutional licenses.

## Key Challenges

### Scientific Language is Hard
Scientific papers use precise, domain-specific language that is extremely difficult for AI to parse correctly. A claim extracted incorrectly can propagate through the knowledge base, creating confident misinformation.

Mitigation: Domain-specific models trained on expert-annotated corpora. Explicit uncertainty scores — the system does not pretend to understand things it does not. Expert researcher community who can flag extraction errors.

### The Paywalled Literature Problem
A significant fraction of the scientific literature is behind publisher paywalls. This creates a knowledge gap that skews the compiled knowledge base toward open-access papers.

Mitigation: Publisher partnership program (see business model). Interlibrary loan integrations. Legal fair-use processing where applicable. Over time, open access mandates from funders will solve this problem.

### Scientific Politics
Researchers and institutions will object to automated quality scoring of their work. A paper assigned a low evidence quality score by an AI will generate complaints from authors.

Mitigation: Transparent, auditable scoring criteria. Appeal mechanism where researchers can contest their paper's characterization. Scores are based on publicly auditable methodology dimensions, not subjective AI judgment.

### Responsibility for Errors
When the platform confidently summarizes the evidence on a medical question and a physician makes a treatment decision based on that summary, what is the legal responsibility if the summary was wrong?

Mitigation: Clear terms of service limiting liability. Explicit uncertainty communication (never present a synthesis as more confident than the evidence warrants). Partnership with medical professional bodies for endorsement of appropriate use standards.

## Starting Point

### First Domain: Clinical Medicine

The most compelling and commercially valuable first market is clinical medicine. The evidence base for medical decisions is enormous, the cost of getting it wrong is high, and there are paying institutional customers (hospitals, health systems, pharma companies) with clear budgets.

Start with a focused question type: "What is the evidence for treating condition X with intervention Y?" This is the question asked by millions of physicians every day. The answer is currently found by searching PubMed, reading abstracts, and hoping someone wrote a Cochrane review.

Build a demonstrably better answer generator for this question type. Get it validated by clinical experts. Publish a validation study showing that your answers match what expert clinicians would conclude from manual review.

This validation study is the marketing for every institutional sale.

## The Deeper Argument

Science is the most reliable method humanity has ever developed for understanding reality. But its infrastructure — the journal system, the peer review process, the replication apparatus — was designed for an era of physical paper and manual calculation. It has not fundamentally changed in 300 years.

The Science Compiler is the infrastructure upgrade that science has been waiting for. It does not change the scientific method. It makes the scientific method actually work at scale, with the transparency and reproducibility that peer review was always supposed to guarantee but never could.

A world where every claim can be traced to its evidence, every analysis can be reproduced, and every synthesis is explicit and contestable is not just a better world for scientists. It is a better world for democracy, public health, and human decision-making at every level.

That is not a startup pitch. That is a description of civilization's upgrade.
